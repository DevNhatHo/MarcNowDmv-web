# WEB-013 — Reconcile trip-level and stop-level official status

Status: NOT_STARTED

## Goal

Stop the detail screen appearing to contradict itself when the operator publishes stop-level
delays but no trip-level status.

## Why

Observed in live MDOT MTA data on 2026-09-30. Train548 rendered "Realtime status
unavailable" as its dominant claim while, two blocks below, the delay trend read "Measured
against the operator's published delay of 1 min 2 sec late". Both statements are correct —
the top-level `status` is derived only from trip-level Trip Update evidence, and the trend is
measured from stop-level delays — but together they read as the page disagreeing with
itself, which costs the trust every other rule on this screen is built to earn.

No synthetic fixture caught this, because every fixture paired a trip-level delay with its
stop-level one. Real MDOT data does not: the feed publishes `StopTimeUpdate` delays with no
trip-level `schedule_relationship` or delay at all. This is therefore the **normal** case for
MARC, not an edge case.

## Dependencies

[WEB-005](WEB-005.md), [WEB-007](WEB-007.md).

## Scope

Decide and implement what a commuter sees when stop-level official evidence exists and
trip-level evidence does not. Apply the decision consistently to the detail screen's dominant
status, the list row's status and the Pulse line summary, so one train is not described three
different ways. Record the decision and its reasoning in DESIGN.md's status language table.

## Out of Scope

Deriving a trip-level status in the browser from stop-level delays, changing the backend's
`status` field, inventing a delay figure, map work, and any change to how the trend itself is
calculated.

## Expected Files

lib/presentation/status.ts, components/TrainDetailScreen.tsx, components/TrainRow.tsx,
lib/presentation/pulse.ts, docs/DESIGN.md, docs/API_CONTRACT.md, existing tests.

## Implementation Notes

The backend's top-level `status` stays authoritative for what it claims; this ticket changes
**presentation**, not derivation. The honest framings worth weighing are: keep "Realtime
status unavailable" dominant but explain in the same breath that stop-level reports exist;
or lead with what *is* known at stop level while stating plainly that no overall status was
published. Whichever is chosen, a stop-level delay must never be relabelled as a trip status,
and "on time" must not appear for a train the operator has not declared on time.

`officialStopUpdates` already carries the per-stop delays, and
`calculated.officialDelayTrend.officialDelaySeconds` carries the figure the trend measured
against. Neither is new data; only the wording around them changes.

Check the Pulse line summary while here: `isCurrentClaim` counts a train as unreported when
its status is UNKNOWN, so a line whose trains all publish stop-level delays currently reads
"not currently reporting status for any of them". Decide whether that remains the honest
sentence.

## Acceptance Criteria

A train with stop-level delays and no trip-level status reads coherently on detail, in its
list row and in its line summary, with no sentence appearing to contradict another. No
fabricated trip status or delay appears anywhere. A train with neither kind of evidence still
reads as unavailable. The live MDOT shape — stop-level delays, no trip-level status — is
covered by a fixture derived from a real capture, not invented.

## Tests Required

Test the live shape (stop-level delays present, trip-level absent), trip-level present,
neither present, and both present; the list row and Pulse summary for each; and that no
rendering produces "on time" without a trip-level ON_TIME. Run all established checks.
